# Phase B: Synthetic Media Responsible Use Policy

## Organizational Context: University Communications Office

This policy is designed for a university communications office responsible for producing and distributing official institutional communications to students, faculty, staff, prospective students, alumni, families, and the public. The office may produce website content, social media posts, videos, audio messages, informational campaigns, emergency-related communications, event promotions, and other digital materials.

Synthetic media could provide legitimate benefits in this setting, including narration for informational content, accessibility support, translation, localization, and efficient production of routine communications. However, university communications also carry institutional authority. Audiences may reasonably assume that an official message represents the university or the individual whose voice or likeness appears in it. For that reason, synthetic media used in this setting requires clear boundaries around truthfulness, identity, consent, disclosure, review, and distribution.

This policy applies to employees, student workers, contractors, vendors, and other individuals creating synthetic media on behalf of the university communications office.


## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to establish clear rules for the responsible creation, use, approval, and distribution of synthetic media by the University Communications Office.

Synthetic media may improve accessibility, production efficiency, localization, and communication quality, but it can also create confusion about who is speaking, whether an event actually occurred, and whether an individual has endorsed a message. The purpose of this policy is therefore not to prohibit synthetic media entirely, but to define when its use is appropriate, when additional review is required, and when the technology must not be used.

All synthetic media produced under this policy must have a legitimate university purpose, use accurate source information, respect the identity and consent of represented individuals, and be disclosed clearly enough that a reasonable audience can understand that synthetic generation or significant AI modification was involved.


## 2. Scope

This policy applies to synthetic audio, video, images, avatars, voice cloning, digitally generated presenters, and other media created or substantially altered using generative artificial intelligence.

It applies when synthetic media is created by:

- University Communications Office employees
- Student employees working for the office
- Contractors or vendors producing content for the office
- Other university personnel acting on behalf of the communications office

The policy applies to content distributed through university websites, social media accounts, email, messaging systems, presentations, digital signage, advertisements, videos, podcasts, and other official communication channels.

Minor technical editing such as noise reduction, basic color correction, captioning, or ordinary image resizing is not considered synthetic representation under this policy unless the modification materially changes what a person appears to have said, done, or looked like.


## 3. Definitions

**Synthetic media:** Audio, video, images, or other media generated or materially altered using artificial intelligence in a way that creates or modifies a representation of a person, event, environment, or communication.

**Synthetic voice:** An AI-generated voice that does not represent an identifiable real person's voice.

**Voice clone:** A synthetic voice designed to reproduce or closely imitate the recognizable voice of a real person.

**Synthetic likeness:** An AI-generated or altered visual representation that reproduces or closely resembles an identifiable real person.

**Disclosure:** A clear notice informing the audience that content was generated or materially altered using artificial intelligence.

**Provenance:** Information documenting how content was created, modified, approved, and distributed.

**High-risk synthetic media:** Synthetic content involving identifiable individuals, official institutional authority, emergency or safety information, sensitive topics, large-scale automated distribution, or circumstances in which audiences could reasonably mistake synthetic representation for authentic human communication.


## 4. Permitted Uses

Synthetic media may be used by the University Communications Office when it serves a legitimate institutional purpose, does not misrepresent the actions or statements of a real person, and complies with the consent, disclosure, review, and provenance requirements of this policy.

The following uses are permitted:

- **Generic synthetic narration:** AI-generated voices that do not imitate an identifiable real person may be used to narrate informational videos, tutorials, orientation materials, event information, and similar university content.

- **Accessibility support:** Synthetic speech may be used to provide audio versions of written university information or other accessibility-focused content when the underlying information has been reviewed for accuracy.

- **Translation and localization:** Synthetic voices or avatars may be used to communicate approved university information in additional languages when the translated content has been reviewed for accuracy before publication.

- **Routine informational content:** Synthetic media may be used for non-sensitive communications such as explanations of university services, campus resources, procedures, deadlines, or general promotional information.

- **Creative or illustrative content:** Clearly fictional synthetic images, voices, or characters may be used in educational or promotional materials when a reasonable audience would not mistake them for documentation of a real person or real event.

- **Synthetic representation of a consenting individual:** A real person's voice or likeness may be synthetically reproduced only when the individual has provided documented, use-specific consent and the proposed use has received the required approval under this policy.

Permitted status does not eliminate other requirements in this policy. Content involving an identifiable real person, sensitive subject matter, large-scale automated distribution, or significant institutional authority may require additional review even when the underlying use would otherwise be permitted.


## 5. Prohibited Uses

The University Communications Office will not create, commission, publish, or knowingly distribute synthetic media for any of the following purposes, regardless of whether the content could be labeled as AI-generated:

- **Fabricated official events or statements:** Synthetic media must not depict an event that did not occur or present words as having been spoken by a person who did not actually make or authorize the statement.

- **Unauthorized voice or likeness cloning:** The recognizable voice, face, likeness, or other identifiable characteristics of a real person must not be synthetically reproduced without documented consent.

- **Deceptive impersonation:** Synthetic media must not be designed to make an audience believe that a real university official, faculty member, student, employee, public figure, or other identifiable person personally created or delivered a communication when they did not.

- **Fabricated testimonials or endorsements:** Synthetic media must not create a false impression that a student, employee, alumnus, faculty member, or other person endorsed the university, a program, a product, a position, or an event.

- **False documentary evidence:** Synthetic images, audio, or video must not be presented as authentic documentation of a real campus event, incident, individual, location, or historical occurrence when the depicted material was artificially generated.

- **Synthetic emergency communications impersonating a real person:** Voice clones, synthetic likenesses, or fabricated recordings of university leaders or emergency personnel must not be used to communicate emergency, safety, security, evacuation, or crisis instructions.

- **Removal or intentional concealment of required disclosure:** Employees may not knowingly remove, obscure, crop, or alter required synthetic-media disclosures or provenance information for the purpose of making synthetic content appear authentic.

- **Content intended to deceive, harass, intimidate, defame, or manipulate:** Synthetic media must not be used to deliberately mislead an audience or cause harm to an individual or group.

- **Unapproved automated mass personalization:** The office must not deploy systems that automatically generate and distribute large volumes of personalized synthetic audio, video, or other media without prior review and authorization.

When there is uncertainty about whether a proposed use falls within a prohibited category, production must stop until the designated reviewer evaluates the request. Disclosure does not convert a prohibited use into a permitted one.


## 6. Consent Requirements

When synthetic media uses the recognizable voice, likeness, face, or other identifiable characteristics of a real person, documented consent must be obtained before production begins.

The following requirements apply:

- **Consent must be obtained in advance:** The employee or project owner responsible for the synthetic-media project must obtain permission before creating a voice clone, synthetic likeness, or other identifiable representation.

- **Consent must be specific to the intended use:** Permission must identify the purpose of the content, the type of synthetic representation being created, the intended audience, the channels through which it will be distributed, and the expected duration of use.

- **General appearance or media releases are not sufficient:** Existing permission to photograph, record, or feature an individual does not automatically authorize creation of a synthetic version of that person's voice or likeness.

- **Consent must be documented:** Written or electronically recorded consent must be retained by the University Communications Office before publication.

- **New uses require new permission:** Consent for one project does not authorize substantially different future uses. For example, permission to use a synthetic voice in an instructional video does not automatically permit its use in advertising, fundraising, emergency communication, or another unrelated campaign.

- **Consent may be withdrawn for future use:** An individual may revoke permission for new synthetic-media uses. Previously distributed materials may not always be completely recoverable, but the office must stop future publication or reuse when revocation becomes effective.

- **Minors require appropriate authorization:** Synthetic representation of a minor requires documented permission from the appropriate parent or legal guardian in addition to any other university requirements.

If valid consent cannot be obtained or documented, the identifiable person's synthetic voice or likeness must not be used.


## 7. Disclosure Requirements

Synthetic media produced by the University Communications Office must be disclosed clearly when a reasonable audience could otherwise believe that the represented voice, image, video, person, or event is authentic.

The following disclosure standards apply:

- **Synthetic audio:** The accompanying page, post, or description must clearly state that the voice was generated using artificial intelligence. For stand-alone audio or content likely to be separated from its original page, a spoken disclosure should also be included when practical.

- **Synthetic video or avatars:** A visible notice such as "AI-generated" or "synthetic media" must appear in the video or immediately adjacent to it.

- **Synthetic images:** Images that could reasonably be mistaken for authentic photographs or documentation must be labeled as AI-generated or synthetic.

- **Real-person voice or likeness:** Content involving an authorized synthetic representation of an identifiable person must disclose that the representation was generated or materially altered using AI, even when consent has been obtained.

- **Re-shareable content:** When content is likely to be downloaded, clipped, reposted, or distributed outside university-controlled channels, the disclosure should be embedded in or attached directly to the media whenever technically practical rather than relying only on a webpage caption or filename.

- **Disclosure must be understandable:** Labels must use plain language and should not rely solely on technical terms, metadata, or symbols that an ordinary audience may not recognize.

Employees and contractors may not intentionally remove, hide, or minimize required disclosures in order to make synthetic content appear authentic.

Disclosure does not establish that content is accurate, consensual, or otherwise permitted. A prohibited use remains prohibited even when it is clearly labeled as synthetic.


## 8. Provenance and Content Credentials

The University Communications Office must maintain reasonable provenance information for synthetic media produced under this policy so that the origin and approval history of an artifact can be reconstructed when necessary.

For each synthetic-media project, the office should retain, when applicable:

- The original source material used to create the content
- The AI or synthetic-media tool used
- The date of creation
- The employee, contractor, or vendor responsible for production
- The final approved version of the content
- Required consent records
- The identity of the person who reviewed or approved publication
- Information about significant modifications made after generation

When the production tool supports content credentials, cryptographic provenance, watermarking, or standards such as C2PA, those features should be preserved when technically practical.

Provenance information should not be treated as proof that the content is truthful or ethical. Metadata and content credentials may be removed during downloading, editing, re-encoding, screenshotting, or redistribution. For that reason, provenance must operate alongside disclosure, review, and other safeguards rather than replacing them.


## 9. Review and Approval Process

Synthetic-media projects must receive a level of review appropriate to their potential risk before publication.

The following review process applies:

- **Routine low-risk uses:** Generic synthetic narration, accessibility content, and routine informational material that does not represent an identifiable real person may be approved through the office's normal editorial review process. The reviewer must confirm the accuracy of the underlying information and compliance with disclosure requirements.

- **Use of a real person's identity:** Any synthetic use of an identifiable person's voice, likeness, or other recognizable characteristics requires documented consent and approval from a designated communications supervisor before production or publication.

- **High-risk synthetic media:** Projects involving sensitive subjects, institutional leadership, large-scale automated distribution, or circumstances in which audiences may reasonably mistake synthetic content for authentic human communication require additional review by the communications director or another designated senior reviewer.

- **Accuracy review:** The reviewer must verify material factual claims against the approved source information before publication. Synthetic generation must not be used to invent facts, quotations, events, endorsements, or statements.

- **Disclosure review:** The reviewer must confirm that required AI-generated or synthetic-media disclosures are present and reasonably likely to remain understandable in the intended distribution format.

- **Final artifact review:** Approval must apply to the final version that will actually be published. Significant changes made after approval require another review.

A reviewer must reject or return a project for revision when required consent, disclosure, provenance information, or factual verification is missing. When the reviewer cannot confidently determine whether a proposed use complies with this policy, publication must be paused until the issue is resolved.

## 10. Publication and Distribution

Before synthetic media is published or distributed, the University Communications Office must consider how the content may be encountered both within and outside its original context.

The following requirements apply:

- Synthetic content must be distributed through appropriate official university channels when it represents or communicates official university information.

- Required disclosures must appear in the published version and should remain associated with the media when it is downloaded, shared, embedded, or reposted whenever technically practical.

- Employees should not rely solely on filenames or surrounding webpage text for disclosure when the media is likely to circulate independently.

- Content intended for multiple platforms must be checked to ensure that disclosure remains visible or understandable on each platform.

- Significant excerpts or shortened versions created by the office must preserve the disclosure and must not change the meaning of the original content.

- Third-party vendors distributing synthetic media on behalf of the office must follow the same disclosure and approval requirements that apply to university personnel.

If the office reasonably expects that the intended distribution method will remove essential disclosure or create a misleading impression about the source or authenticity of the content, publication must be modified, moved to another format, or declined.


## 11. Incident Response

If synthetic media produced or commissioned by the University Communications Office is published incorrectly, misused, misattributed, altered deceptively, distributed without required disclosure, or reaches an unintended audience in a way that creates a material risk of confusion or harm, the office must respond promptly.

The response should include the following actions when appropriate:

- **Preserve evidence:** Save the original approved artifact, relevant source materials, consent documentation, publication records, and available copies of the altered or misused content.

- **Stop further distribution:** Pause scheduled posts, campaigns, automated distribution, or other university-controlled publication of the affected content while the incident is evaluated.

- **Notify responsible personnel:** Report the incident to the communications director or designated supervisor. Appropriate university offices should also be notified when the incident involves safety, privacy, legal, security, or other specialized concerns.

- **Correct misleading information:** When the university's own publication was inaccurate or insufficiently disclosed, issue a correction or clarification through appropriate channels.

- **Address unauthorized redistribution:** When technically and practically possible, request correction, relabeling, or removal of materially altered or misleading copies distributed by third parties.

- **Assess impact:** Determine what happened, which audiences may have received the content, whether an identifiable individual was affected, and whether additional communication is necessary.

- **Document the incident:** Record the cause of the incident, actions taken, and any changes needed to prevent a similar failure.

The office should not claim that every redistributed copy can be removed. Once synthetic media leaves university-controlled channels, complete recovery may be impossible. Incident response should therefore focus on limiting additional harm, correcting the official record, and learning from the failure.


## 12. Refusal Criteria

The University Communications Office may decline to use synthetic media even when a proposed project appears to fall within a permitted category.

Synthetic media must be refused when:

- The accuracy of important source information cannot be verified.
- Required consent cannot be obtained or documented.
- The proposed representation creates an unreasonable risk that audiences will believe a real person personally spoke, appeared, or endorsed something they did not.
- Required disclosure cannot be presented clearly or is likely to be ineffective in the intended format.
- The project depends on concealing the synthetic nature of the content in order to achieve its intended effect.
- The content involves an emergency, crisis, safety, or similarly sensitive situation where synthetic representation could create unnecessary confusion about the authenticity or authority of the message.
- The available tools cannot produce the content reliably enough for the intended purpose.
- The potential harm from misinterpretation, redistribution, or misuse is greater than the communication benefit provided by using synthetic media.
- A project owner or reviewer cannot determine with reasonable confidence that the proposed use complies with this policy.

When synthetic media is refused, the office should use an appropriate alternative such as authentic human recording, written communication, conventional photography or video, or another non-synthetic format.

Efficiency, lower production cost, convenience, or increased audience engagement alone are not sufficient reasons to override a refusal under this policy.
